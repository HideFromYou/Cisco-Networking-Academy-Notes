# Wireless and Mobile Networks — Summary

## Overview

End-of-module recap (3.3.1 "What Did I Learn in this Module?"). It restates the two topics of the module; the details live in their own sections.

## Recap

- **Wireless Networks** — Mobile phones use **radio waves** to send voice signals to antennas mounted on **towers** in specific geographic areas. A call's voice signal is relayed from tower to tower until it reaches its destination. This network is used for calls to another mobile phone or a wired telephone, and for text messages sent directly from the phone. The most common type of cellular telephone network is the **GSM** network. **3G, 4G, 4G-LTE, and 5G** describe enhanced cell phone networks optimized for fast data transmission; currently **4G still dominates** as the mobile network used by most phones.
  - Besides the GSM and 4G/5G transmitters and receivers, smartphones also connect through **Wi-Fi** (to local networks and the internet; usually privately owned networks, often with guest or public **hotspots**), **Bluetooth** (short-distance communication, several devices at once), and **NFC** (data exchange at very close proximity, usually less than a few centimeters). → see [01-Wireless-Networks](../01-Wireless-Networks/README.md)
- **Mobile Device Connectivity** — Almost all mobile devices can connect to Wi-Fi. Precautions: never send login/password in plaintext, use a VPN for sensitive data, enable security on home networks, and use WPA2 or higher.
  - Android and Apple iOS are the two most popular mobile operating systems. Devices are preprogrammed to use Wi-Fi if one is available and the device can connect to the access point and receive an IP address; otherwise they use cellular data if configured.
  - **Bluetooth** gives a simple way to connect mobile devices to each other and to wireless accessories: wireless, automatic, and very low power. Examples: hands-free headsets, keyboards, a mouse, stereo controls, car speakerphones, and mobile speakers.
  - **Pairing** happens when two Bluetooth devices establish a connection to share resources: the radios are on, one device searches, and the others must be in **discoverable (visible)** mode. A discoverable device transmits its name, Bluetooth class, services it can use, and technical information (features or the Bluetooth specification it supports). → see [02-Mobile-Device-Connectivity](../02-Mobile-Device-Connectivity/README.md)
