# Wireless Networks

## Overview

Covers the different types of networks a smartphone uses besides its cellular (GSM and 4G/5G) transmitters and receivers: GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and NFC.

> The lesson also contains two videos, 3.1.1 *Types of Wireless Networks* and 3.1.2 *Cell Phone Interactions with Different Networks*; their transcripts were not included in the material.

## Learning Objectives

- Describe the different types of networks used by cell phones and mobile devices

## Other Wireless Networks

In addition to the GSM and 4G/5G transmitters and receivers, smartphones connect in a variety of ways:

| Technology | What it does |
|---|---|
| **GPS** (Global Positioning System) | Satellites transmit signals that cover the globe. The phone receives them and calculates its location to within about **10 meters**. |
| **Wi-Fi** | Wi-Fi transmitters and receivers in the phone connect it to local networks and the internet. The phone must be within range of a wireless **access point**. Wi-Fi networks are usually privately owned but often offer guest or public access **hotspots** (an area where Wi-Fi signals are available). A phone's Wi-Fi connection is similar to a laptop's. |
| **Bluetooth** | Low-power, shorter-range wireless technology meant to replace wired connections for accessories such as speakers, headphones, and microphones; can also connect a smartwatch to a smartphone. It carries both data and voice, so it can create small local networks, and multiple devices can be connected at once. |
| **NFC** (Near Field Communication) | Exchanges data between devices in very close proximity, usually **less than a few centimeters** (for example a smartphone and a payment system). Uses **electromagnetic fields** to transmit data. |

## Key Takeaways

- A smartphone uses several wireless technologies besides its cellular radio: GPS for location, Wi-Fi for local networks and the internet, Bluetooth for short-range accessories, and NFC for very-close-range exchanges.
- Wi-Fi needs an access point in range; GPS only receives signals from satellites; NFC works only at a few centimeters.
