# Communications in a Connected World — Summary

## Overview

End-of-module recap (1.4.1 "What Did I Learn in this Module?"). It restates the three topics of the module; the details live in their own sections.

## Recap

- **Network Types** — The internet is not owned by anyone; it is a worldwide collection of interconnected networks cooperating through common standards, over telephone wires, fiber, wireless, and satellite. Small home networks connect a few computers to each other and the internet; SOHO networks let home/remote-office computers reach a corporate network or shared resources; medium-to-large networks (corporations, schools) can have hundreds or thousands of interconnected hosts. Many non-PC devices (mobile, home, smart cars, RFID, sensors, actuators, medical devices) connect too. → see [01-Network-Types](../01-Network-Types/README.md)
- **Data Transmission** — Personal data is volunteered, observed, or inferred. A bit is a binary digit (0 or 1). Signals travel as electrical, optical, or wireless transmissions. → see [02-Data-Transmission](../02-Data-Transmission/README.md)
- **Bandwidth and Throughput** — Bandwidth is a medium's theoretical capacity (Kbps/Mbps/Gbps); throughput is the actual rate, influenced by data volume and latency from the devices along the path. → see [03-Bandwidth-and-Throughput](../03-Bandwidth-and-Throughput/README.md)
