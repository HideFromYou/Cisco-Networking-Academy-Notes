# Network Types

## Overview

Covers what "being online" actually means, who (if anyone) owns the internet, how local networks scale from a couple of home PCs up to worldwide interconnection, and the broad categories of non-PC devices that now connect to networks (mobile, home, and other IoT-style devices).

## Learning Objectives

- Explain what "online" means in the context of the global internet
- Describe who owns/governs the internet
- Distinguish network scales: SOHO, medium-to-large business, worldwide
- Identify categories of internet-connected devices beyond desktops/laptops

## Everything Is Online

"Online" is mostly taken for granted now — phones, tablets, laptops, and desktops are expected to stay connected to the global internet by default. When people say "the internet," they usually don't mean the literal physical wiring/infrastructure — they mean the general "place" where information is found and shared.

## Who Owns "The Internet"?

**No single individual or organization owns the internet.** It's a worldwide collection of interconnected networks (an "internetwork," hence "internet") that cooperate using common standards. Telephone wires, fiber-optic cable, wireless transmission, and satellite links all carry this traffic. Every online destination — social media, games, email, courses — is really just content hosted on a local network that connects out through the internet.

## Local Networks and Scale

Local networks range enormously in size:

| Scale | Description |
|---|---|
| Small Home | A few computers connected to each other and to the internet |
| **SOHO** (Small Office/Home Office) | Lets a handful of local users share resources — printers, documents, pictures, music |
| Medium to Large | Business networks — advertising, sales, ordering supplies, customer communication |
| World Wide | The internet itself — a "network of networks," literally thousands of local networks interconnected |

Business/SOHO networks are usually more efficient and cheaper than traditional communication (mail, long-distance calls), and provide rapid communication (email, IM) plus centralized access to information on network servers.

## Devices Beyond Desktops and Laptops

### Mobile Devices
Smartphone, tablet, smartwatch, smart glasses — smartphones in particular combine what used to be separate products (phone, camera, GPS, media player, computer) into one.

### Connected Home Devices
Security systems, appliances, smart TVs, gaming consoles — monitorable/configurable remotely via a mobile device.

### Other Connected Devices
- **Smart cars** — maps, audio/video, destination info; can text/email on theft or crash; sync with phones/tablets for engine status, maintenance alerts, security status
- **RFID tags** — placed in/on packages for tracking
- **Sensors and actuators**
- **Medical devices**

## Key Takeaways

- The internet has no single owner — it's a cooperative, standards-based interconnection of independently-owned networks.
- Network "type" is really a question of scale (small home → SOHO → medium/large business → worldwide), not a difference in underlying technology.
- The device landscape connecting to networks has expanded far past PCs — mobile, home, and embedded/IoT devices (cars, RFID, sensors, medical devices) are now routine network endpoints, each expanding the practical attack/management surface.
