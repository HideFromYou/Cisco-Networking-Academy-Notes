# Network Technologies in the Home

## Overview

Covers the wireless frequency ranges used by home LAN technologies (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, Bluetooth vs 802.11) and the wired technologies used at home (Ethernet over UTP, coaxial cable, fiber-optic cable, powerline).

## Learning Objectives

- Describe wired and wireless network technologies

## LAN Wireless Frequencies

The wireless technologies most used in home networks are in the **unlicensed 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz** frequency ranges. Certain areas of the electromagnetic spectrum can be used without a permit.

| Technology | Band | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Bluetooth** | 2.4 GHz | Low-speed, short-range. Its advantage is **one-to-many** communication with many devices at the same time, which makes it the preferred method for computer peripherals (wireless mice, keyboards, printers) and good for audio to speakers or headphones. |
| **IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi)** | 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz | Transmits at a **much higher power** than Bluetooth, giving greater range and better throughput. |

**Where wireless technologies sit on the electromagnetic spectrum (figure):**

| Technology | Place in the spectrum |
|---|---|
| Audio | Extremely low, very low, and low |
| AM broadcast | Medium to high |
| Short-wave radio | High |
| FM broadcast | Very high |
| Television | Very high and ultra high |
| Cellular (840 MHz) and NPCS (930 MHz) | Ultra high |
| Cordless phones (902–928 MHz) | Between ultra high and super high |
| IEEE 802.11 b/g/n/ad (2.400–2.4835 GHz) | Between ultra high and super high |
| IEEE 802.11 a/n/ac/ad (5.725–5.850 GHz) | Between ultra high and super high |
| Infrared wireless LAN | Infrared |

Beyond infrared, the spectrum continues with visible light, ultraviolet, and X-rays.

## Wired Network Technologies

Even though many home devices support wireless, some applications still benefit from a **wired switch connection that is not shared** with other users on the network.

The most commonly implemented wired protocol is **Ethernet**, a suite of protocols that lets network devices communicate over a wired LAN connection and can use many different wiring media.

- Directly connected devices use an **Ethernet patch cable**, usually **unshielded twisted pair (UTP)**, which can be bought with **RJ-45** connectors already installed, in various lengths.
- Recently built homes may already have Ethernet jacks wired into the walls.
- Homes without UTP wiring can use other technologies, such as **powerline**, to distribute wired connectivity through the premises.

| Wired technology | Description |
|---|---|
| **Category 5e (UTP)** | The most common wiring in a LAN. The cable has **4 pairs of wires twisted** to reduce electrical interference. |
| **Coaxial cable** | An inner wire surrounded by a tubular insulating layer, then a tubular conducting shield; most coax cables also have an external insulating sheath or jacket. |
| **Fiber-optic cable** | Glass or plastic, about the diameter of a human hair. Carries digital information at very high speeds over long distances, with very high bandwidth for very large amounts of data. |

## Key Takeaways

- Home wireless uses the unlicensed 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands; Bluetooth is low-speed, short-range and one-to-many, while 802.11 uses higher power for more range and throughput.
- Ethernet is the standard wired protocol, normally over Cat 5e UTP patch cables with RJ-45 connectors; powerline can substitute where there is no cabling.
- Coaxial and fiber-optic are other wired media; fiber has very high bandwidth over long distances.
