# Home Network Basics

## Overview

Covers the components of a typical home network (the integrated router plus the many devices that connect to it) and the two kinds of ports on a home router.

> The lesson also contains a video, 4.1.1 *Typical Home Network Setup*; its transcript was not included in the material.

## Learning Objectives

- Describe the components required to build a home network

## Components of a Home Network

Besides the integrated router, many types of devices can connect to a home network:

- Desktop computers
- Gaming systems
- Smart TV systems
- Printers
- Scanners
- Security cameras
- Telephones
- Climate control devices

As new technologies come to market, more household functions will rely on the network for connectivity and control.

**Home WLAN (figure):** the ISP connects to the house; the **router** in the house provides the wireless coverage, and wireless devices such as a smart TV, laptop, gaming console, smartphone, and entertainment system connect to it.

## Typical Home Network Routers

Small business and home routers typically have two primary types of ports:

| Port | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ethernet / LAN ports** | Connect to the **internal switch** part of the router, usually labeled "Ethernet" or "LAN". All devices connected to the switch ports are on the **same local network**. |
| **Internet port** | Connects the router to a **different network** than the Ethernet ports. Often used to connect to the **cable or DSL modem** to reach the internet. |

Many home routers also include a **radio antenna and a built-in wireless access point**. By default, wireless devices are on the **same local network** as the devices plugged into the LAN switch ports. The **internet port is the only port on a different network** in the default configuration.

## Key Takeaways

- An integrated home router combines a switch (LAN ports), a wireless access point, and an internet port.
- Wired LAN-port devices and wireless devices share the same local network by default; only the internet port faces a different network, typically the cable or DSL modem.
- Many kinds of devices connect to a home network, from computers and TVs to cameras and climate control.
