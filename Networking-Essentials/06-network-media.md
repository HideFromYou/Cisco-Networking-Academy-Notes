# Module 06 – Network Media

## 6.0 Introduction

Network media provide the physical or wireless path over which data travels. The three main media types are copper cable, fiber-optic cable, and wireless transmission.

## 6.1 Types of Network Media

- **Copper:** Transmits data as electrical impulses.
- **Fiber-optic:** Uses pulses of light traveling through glass or plastic fibers.
- **Wireless:** Uses modulated electromagnetic waves to carry data through the air.

The choice of media depends on:

- Maximum distance between devices.
- The physical environment in which the media will be installed.
- The amount of data and required transmission speed.
- The cost of the media and its installation.

## 6.2 Copper Cabling

### Twisted-Pair Cable

Twisted-pair copper cable is commonly used in Ethernet LANs.

- It contains four pairs (eight conductors) of copper wires.
- The wires in each pair are twisted together.
- Twisting helps reduce the effects of electromagnetic interference and crosstalk.
- Ethernet twisted-pair cables commonly use an RJ-45-style connector.

**EMI (Electromagnetic Interference):** Electrical noise caused by electromagnetic energy from nearby sources that can interfere with signal transmission.
*(Electrical noise caused by electromagnetic energy from nearby sources that can interfere with signal transmission.)*

For example, nearby high-voltage cables or electrical conductors can introduce noise. This may distort signals, cause transmission errors and retransmissions, and reduce reliability.

Twisting helps because interference tends to affect both conductors in a pair similarly. A differential receiver compares the signals on the two conductors, allowing much of the common-mode noise to be canceled.

### Coaxial Cable

Coaxial cable consists of:

- A central copper conductor.
- Insulating material around the conductor.
- A braided metallic shield.
- An outer protective jacket.

It is used in applications such as cable television, satellite connections, and broadband services. Common connector types include BNC and F-type connectors.

## 6.3 Fiber-Optic Cabling

Fiber-optic cable transmits data using light.

Its main components include:

- **Core:** The central region through which light travels.
- **Cladding:** The surrounding layer that keeps light contained within the core.

Characteristics and uses:

- Supports high bandwidth.
- Can carry data over long distances.
- Is not affected by electromagnetic interference in the way copper cabling is.
- Is used in network backbones, enterprise networks, data centers, and telecommunications.

Fiber requires specialized termination and splicing equipment and must be handled carefully.

## Key Takeaways

- Copper uses electrical impulses, fiber uses light pulses, and wireless uses electromagnetic waves.
- Media selection depends on distance, environment, required data rate, and cost.
- Twisted-pair cable is widely used for Ethernet LANs and contains four twisted pairs.
- Twisting helps reduce EMI and crosstalk; differential signaling helps reject common-mode noise.
- Coaxial cable uses a central conductor and shielding and is common in broadband and television services.
- Fiber-optic cable offers high bandwidth, long-distance transmission, and immunity to EMI, but requires specialized handling and installation.
